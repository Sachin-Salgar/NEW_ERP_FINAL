# AI Platform Architecture

## Purpose

The AI Platform provides governed enterprise AI capabilities that can augment ERP users and business processes. It includes AI/ML services, document intelligence, model lifecycle management, retrieval/assistant capabilities, and AI governance.

The platform augments human decision-making. High-impact financial, legal, regulatory, safety, security, and other controlled decisions remain subject to explicit business authorization and human oversight where required.

## Architectural Position

- AI is a platform capability within the current modular-monolith architecture unless an approved ADR introduces an independently deployed AI component.
- Business modules remain authoritative for their domain data and business transactions.
- AI consumes approved APIs, events, documents, and governed data; it must not bypass module boundaries.
- Security, authorization, privacy, tenant isolation, and audit requirements are inherited from the canonical security architecture.
- Training-data governance remains aligned with the canonical data-governance architecture.
- No particular model vendor, cloud provider, local model, vector database, or AI service is mandatory unless explicitly approved elsewhere.

## 1. AI Capability Areas

The platform may provide:

- Predictive analytics.
- Classification.
- Anomaly detection.
- Recommendation systems.
- Generative AI.
- Natural-language processing.
- Computer vision.
- Speech processing.
- Retrieval-augmented knowledge access.
- Document intelligence/OCR.
- Enterprise assistants and copilots.
- Model lifecycle/MLOps capabilities.

These are architectural capabilities, not claims that every capability is already implemented.

## 2. Document Intelligence

Document Intelligence converts supported unstructured or semi-structured inputs into structured information for review and approved business processing.

Potential inputs include scanned documents, PDFs, images, email attachments, office documents, multi-page documents, barcodes, and QR codes.

An illustrative pipeline is:

```text
Document Input
    ↓
Pre-processing
    ↓
OCR / Extraction
    ↓
Classification
    ↓
Confidence / AI Validation
    ↓
Business Validation
    ↓
Human Review where required
    ↓
Approved Business Action
```

Extraction may include text, tables, key-value pairs, dates, amounts, addresses, identifiers, and other configured fields.

Extracted data must be treated as untrusted until applicable validation succeeds. Confidence scores do not replace business validation.

Document Intelligence integrates with Document Management, Workflow, Search, Master Data, Business Rules, BI, and business modules through published contracts.

## 3. AI Architecture

A logical flow may be:

```text
ERP / Platform Data
       ↓
Governed Data Access
       ↓
AI / Retrieval Services
       ↓
Inference / Reasoning
       ↓
Recommendation or Generated Result
       ↓
Validation / Authorization
       ↓
Human or Approved System Action
```

AI output is not authoritative merely because it was generated by a model.

## 4. AI Integration

AI may integrate with Finance, Procurement, Inventory, Sales, CRM, Manufacturing, HR, Quality, Asset Maintenance, BI, Workflow, Document Management, Search, and other approved platform capabilities.

Integration must use standardized APIs, events, or other approved contracts.

AI must not directly update another module's private persistence. Where an AI recommendation results in an ERP transaction, the authoritative module must validate and execute that transaction.

## 5. Model Lifecycle / MLOps

Where machine-learning models are implemented, the lifecycle may include:

1. Data preparation.
2. Feature engineering where applicable.
3. Training.
4. Validation.
5. Testing.
6. Approval.
7. Deployment.
8. Monitoring.
9. Retraining.
10. Retirement.

A model registry may maintain model identity, version, owner, training-data reference, algorithm/model family, approval status, deployment status, and performance information.

Deployment may support batch or real-time inference where required. Streaming inference, canary deployment, A/B testing, and automatic rollback are optional capabilities and require an approved implementation design.

Model monitoring may include accuracy, precision/recall where meaningful, drift, latency, resource usage, and failure rate.

## 6. Enterprise AI Assistants and Copilots

Assistants may provide:

- Natural-language queries.
- Enterprise knowledge retrieval.
- Document summarization.
- Workflow assistance.
- Report explanation.
- Recommendations.
- Guided data entry.
- Task assistance.

Capabilities are individually enableable and must respect the user's actual authorization.

An assistant must not reveal information merely because it can technically retrieve it. Retrieval must be security-trimmed to the requesting user's permitted tenant, organization, records, documents, and actions.

Generated actions must be explicit and validated by the authoritative business module before execution. High-impact actions may require confirmation or human approval.

Conversation history and retention must follow enterprise privacy and retention policy.

## 7. AI Governance and Security

AI implementations must address, as applicable:

- Authorization.
- Tenant isolation.
- Data classification.
- Sensitive-data handling.
- Auditability.
- Model/version traceability.
- Human oversight.
- Prompt/instruction integrity.
- Output validation.
- Retention.
- Responsible-AI requirements.

Training or evaluation data must use governed sources and approved data-access mechanisms.

No AI feature may weaken existing authorization or expose another user's/tenant's data.

## 8. AI Reporting and Monitoring

Platform monitoring may include:

- AI feature usage.
- Model performance.
- Inference latency.
- Failure rates.
- Drift.
- Recommendation outcomes where measurable.
- Human corrections.
- Assistant quality signals.

Monitoring data itself remains subject to security and privacy requirements.

## 9. Extensibility

Future AI capabilities may include additional models, agentic assistance, multimodal processing, intelligent mapping, or domain-specific models. Such capabilities require architectural and security review before becoming implementation requirements.

## 10. Implementation Rules for AI/Copilot

AI-assisted development must:

- Reuse existing platform and module contracts.
- Never invent an AI provider, model, dataset, or deployment dependency.
- Never treat generated output as authoritative business data without validation.
- Never bypass authorization for retrieval or action execution.
- Keep experimental/future AI capabilities separate from implemented functionality.
- Preserve auditability for AI-assisted high-impact actions.
- STOP and ask when model ownership, data access, security, business authority, or required human approval is unclear.

## Summary

The AI Platform provides governed, extensible AI capabilities for the ERP while preserving domain ownership, security, human oversight, and the distinction between AI recommendations and authoritative ERP transactions.
